Salt Lake City airport to Park City

Thirty-two miles, one canyon, and the question of whether you need a car at all.

Guide · Updated October 2026

Salt Lake City International is the closest major airport to Park City at roughly 32 miles, and in clear conditions the drive takes about 45 minutes. That proximity is a large part of why Park City draws the volume it does: you can land at midday and be on a chairlift the same afternoon, which is not true of most western US ski towns.

The decision worth thinking about before you book is whether you need a car at all.

The route

It is almost entirely motorway. I-80 east out of the airport, through Parley’s Canyon, then exit 145 or 146 for Park City. There is one climb of real consequence, Parley’s Summit at 7,020 feet, and that is the part that behaves differently in winter.

Shuttle or rental car

This is the question most people get wrong, usually by defaulting to a rental car because that is what they do everywhere else.

Where a shuttle wins

If you are staying somewhere with ski access and eating mostly in or on the resort, a car will sit in a parking space for a week while you pay for it. Shared shuttles run continuously from the airport and drop at individual properties. In a canyon snowstorm, a driver who makes the run several times a day is a better bet than a visitor in an unfamiliar rental.

Park City also runs a free public bus around the town and the resort areas, which covers most of what visitors actually do.

Where a car wins

If you intend to ski more than one mountain, a car starts to earn its place. Deer Valley, Solitude, Brighton, Snowbird and Alta are all within reasonable reach and the bus network does not cover them conveniently. Big grocery runs for a group are also much easier with a car than on a shuttle.

If you do rent

  • Take all-wheel drive or four-wheel drive. Utah enforces traction restrictions in the canyons during storms, and a two-wheel-drive car on all-season tyres can be turned back.
  • Check what the tyres actually are. All-wheel drive on summer tyres is not winter capable, and rental fleets vary.
  • Confirm parking before you arrive. Several Park City properties charge for it or have none, and in February the overflow fills.

Flying in on the day you want to ski

It is genuinely possible here in a way it is not in most ski destinations. A morning flight landing before noon usually means afternoon skiing, if two things are true: you have your gear sorted and you are not stopping to shop.

Gear is the easier of the two. Several Park City resorts have rental shops on the property, which removes a town detour on your first morning. Groceries are the one that catches people, because the supermarkets are in town rather than at the resorts, and a big shop with a tired group after a flight is nobody’s idea of a holiday. Properties with a market on site make the first evening considerably simpler.

Winter driving, honestly

Parley’s Canyon is a well-maintained interstate, not a mountain pass in the alarming sense, and it is ploughed constantly. But it climbs 2,000 feet in a few miles, and conditions at the summit are frequently different from conditions at the airport.

During an active storm, expect the drive to take roughly twice as long. Occasionally the canyon closes outright for avalanche control or a crash. It is worth having a plan that does not depend on arriving at an exact hour, particularly on the first day.

Check udottraffic.utah.gov before setting off. It carries live camera feeds and current restrictions, and in winter it is more useful than any mapping app estimate.

Other airports

Salt Lake City is the practical answer for almost everyone, but for completeness: Provo is about 70 minutes away with a limited schedule, and Heber Valley is a general aviation field roughly 25 minutes out with no commercial service. Neither is a reason to skip SLC, which has direct flights from most major US cities.

The short version

  • 32 miles, about 45 minutes in clear weather, double that in a storm
  • I-80 east through Parley’s Canyon, exit 145 or 146
  • Skip the car if you are staying with ski access and not touring other resorts
  • If you rent, take AWD or 4WD and check the tyres
  • Park City’s town bus is free and covers most visitor routes
  • Check udottraffic.utah.gov before driving in winter
