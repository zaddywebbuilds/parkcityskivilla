A Two-Bedroom Villa or Two Hotel Rooms: The Honest Comparison

Published 29 September 2026 Park City, Utah 6 min read

Two hotel rooms and one two-bedroom villa can land within a few dollars of each other per night. The nightly rate is the least interesting part of the comparison.

On this page
  1. Where the money actually goes
  2. Breakfast is the big one
  3. The evenings
  4. When hotel rooms are the right answer

For a family of six to eight, this is usually the real decision, and it gets made on a price comparison that leaves out most of what matters.

Where the money actually goes

Start with the nightly rate, then add the things that follow from each choice.

A seven night stay for a group of eight.
Two-bedroom villaTwo or three hotel rooms
SleepingTwo bedrooms plus sleeper sofas, one unitSplit across rooms, possibly on different floors
BreakfastIn the kitchen, at your own paceRestaurant or room service, every morning
EveningsOne living room everybody sharesSomebody's bedroom, or the lobby bar
LaundryIn the unitHotel service, priced per item
Dinner flexibilityCook in when nobody wants to go outOut, or room service
Cost shapeOne rate, plus a grocery shopTwo or three rates, plus every meal out

Breakfast is the big one

Eight people eating breakfast out for seven days is a serious number, and it is a number that does not appear anywhere in the lodging comparison. Add a few dinners cooked in and the gap between the two options stops being close.

The kitchen guide goes through this properly, including the part most people miss, which is that cooking in on a ski trip is not a sacrifice when everybody is tired.

The evenings

This is the part people do not anticipate. In two hotel rooms, a group of eight has nowhere to be together after dinner. You end up sitting on beds, or in a lobby, or splitting up at eight o'clock.

A shared living room is most of why people describe a villa trip differently afterwards. It is also why groups who book it once tend to keep booking it.

When hotel rooms are the right answer

They genuinely are, sometimes. Two couples who want separation rather than togetherness, a short trip where nobody will cook, or a group that wants daily housekeeping and a front desk, are all better served by a hotel.

The villa argument is strongest for families with children, longer stays, and groups who were planning to spend the evenings together anyway.

Compare on the total, not the rate

Work out the all-in cost of each option for your actual group and your actual dates, including every fee, and add a realistic food budget to each. The per-night numbers rarely survive that comparison intact.

Common questions

Is a two-bedroom villa cheaper than two hotel rooms?

Often similar on the nightly rate and meaningfully cheaper in total, because a kitchen removes most of the cost of feeding a group. The comparison only works if you include food and fees rather than just the room rate.

How many people does a two-bedroom villa sleep?

It varies. A Premier Two-Bedroom Villa at Westgate Park City has two king bedrooms and two queen sleeper sofas, sleeping up to eight in 1,100 square feet.

When are hotel rooms the better choice?

For short stays, groups who want separation rather than a shared space, or anyone who values daily housekeeping and a front desk over a kitchen and a living room.
