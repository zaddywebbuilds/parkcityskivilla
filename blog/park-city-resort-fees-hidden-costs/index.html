Resort Fees and the Costs That Appear at Checkout

Published 2 October 2026 Updated 28 November 2026 Park City, Utah

None of these charges are a scandal. They are standard across the industry. The problem is that they are usually invisible while you are comparing options and fully visible once you have chosen.

Comparing accommodation on the nightly rate is how almost everybody shops, and it is the reason the final figure so often comes as a surprise. Park City is not unusual in this regard — the same pattern repeats at ski resorts across the country — but the fee totals here are significant enough that understanding them in advance changes how you compare options.

What gets added

The charges that typically sit between the nightly rate and the total you actually pay fall into six categories. Not all properties carry all of them, and the amounts vary between properties, but most Park City resort accommodation will include several.

Common charges added to nightly accommodation rates in Park City resort properties.
ChargeHow it’s appliedNotes
Resort feePer night, per unitCovers pools, gym, resort shuttles, wifi; charged whether or not used
Village assessmentPer night, per unitFunds communal village infrastructure; a smaller charge separate from the resort fee
Cleaning feeOne-off per bookingScales with unit size; larger on bigger villas
Lodging taxesPercentage of everything aboveApplied to fees as well as the nightly rate
ParkingPer night or per stayRarely included; often added separately at checkout
Platform service feePercentage added by booking siteNot charged by the property; avoided entirely when booking direct

The resort fee and village assessment are set by the resort and apply regardless of which channel you book through. The platform service fee does not: it is added by third-party listing sites and disappears when you book direct from the owner. When fees appear in the booking

The timing of disclosure varies significantly between booking platforms and between properties. Some channels show a full breakdown before you enter payment details. Others show the nightly rate prominently throughout the browsing and selection process, with the fee total appearing only on the final checkout screen.

The most common sequence that catches people off guard: you compare five properties on nightly rate, choose one, work through the booking, and encounter fees totalling several hundred dollars on the final confirmation screen, at the point where changing your mind means restarting the search. At that stage the comparison you made no longer holds, because the properties you did not choose may have had lower fees — or none.

The fix is straightforward but requires a step most people skip. Before comparing any options, ask each property for the total for your exact dates including every fee, charge and tax. That figure is the only one that is comparable.

Why it distorts comparisons

A fee structure that is flat per night affects a cheaper unit proportionally more than an expensive one. Two options that look twenty dollars apart on the nightly rate can land much closer together, or swap places entirely, once everything is included. The cleaning fee introduces a further distortion: a one-off charge looks small relative to the nightly rate on a long stay and large on a short one, so the same property appears differently priced depending on how long you book.

These charges also attach to the unit rather than to the party. That means the total does not divide equally just because the group is sharing it; somebody has to front the cleaning fee and fees before collecting contributions. For a booking split between two households, our guide to splitting a villa between two families covers who pays what and how to handle the reconciliation.

This is not hypothetical. The mismatch between the displayed rate and the amount paid is the single most common reason people feel they overpaid for a ski week, and it is entirely avoidable with one question asked at the right time.

Group bookings

For a large group booking a villa, the per-person picture changes materially once fees are in the total. A nightly fee that looks significant on one person’s share looks smaller spread across eight. At the same time, a cleaning fee sized for a large unit — which is proportionally larger than on a small unit — divides down to something modest when eight or more people are splitting it.

The result is that the total-per-person for a villa that sleeps eight, including all fees, can look quite different from a headline rate comparison. This is the same arithmetic that applies to lift access, groceries and transfers on a ski week: costs that look large as absolutes divide down quickly across a large group. One other thing to confirm for a group: some properties require a security deposit in addition to the total shown at checkout, held against damage and released after checkout. Ask for the deposit amount and the release timeline before booking, particularly for large groups.

Fees are not automatically bad

A resort fee that covers pools, a gym and shuttles you will actually use during a ski week is a reasonable way to price a resort. The objection is to discovering it at checkout, not to its existence.

Equally, a low nightly rate with high fees is not automatically worse than a high nightly rate with none. Only the total tells you which is which. Some properties with high resort fees are also among the best-positioned for ski-in access, the most fully staffed, or the most flexible on check-in timing. The fee is part of the proposition, not evidence against it.

What is worth checking is whether the facilities the resort fee covers are ones you will use. If the group is skiing all day, skipping the fitness centre, and not using resort shuttles because you have a car, the fee covers less than it would for a family using the pool each evening. Neither scenario makes the fee wrong; it just informs whether the property is the right fit. The question to ask

The question to ask every time

“What is the total I will pay for these exact dates, including every fee, charge and tax?” Ask it of every option before comparing any of them. A property that cannot answer quickly is telling you something useful.

Ask it before you have committed to any option, because the answer should inform your choice rather than confirm it. The same question applies whether you are booking through a platform or direct from an owner: the platform fee disappears in the latter case, but the resort fee and any other property charges remain.

Across seven nights, a nightly fee is seven times larger than it looks on the booking page. Fold it into the lodging line of your trip budget rather than treating it as an extra. Common questions

What is a resort fee?

A nightly charge covering shared facilities such as pools, a fitness centre, shuttles and wifi. It is charged regardless of whether you use them, and it is standard across much of the resort industry.

Do Park City rentals charge resort fees?

Many do, often alongside a village or community assessment. They are normal, but they are not always shown until checkout, so ask for the all-in total for your dates before you book.

How do I compare lodging prices fairly?

Ask every option for one number: the total for your exact dates including all fees, charges and tax. Nightly rates are not comparable because fee structures differ between properties.

What is a village assessment fee?

A smaller nightly charge applied in some resort villages, in addition to the main resort fee. It typically funds maintenance of communal village infrastructure and is charged per unit per night. It is distinct from a resort fee and may appear as a separate line item.

Does booking direct avoid the platform service fee?

Yes. When you book direct with the owner rather than through a listing platform such as Vrbo or Airbnb, the platform service fee does not apply. The resort fee and village assessment are set by the resort and apply regardless of booking method.
