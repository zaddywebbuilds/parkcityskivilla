Resort Fees and the Costs That Appear at Checkout

Published 2 October 2026 Park City, Utah 5 min read

None of these charges are a scandal. They are standard across the industry. The problem is that they are usually invisible while you are comparing options and fully visible once you have chosen.

On this page
  1. What gets added
  2. Why it distorts comparisons
  3. Fees are not automatically bad
  4. Put it in the budget

Comparing accommodation on the nightly rate is how almost everybody shops, and it is the reason the final figure so often comes as a surprise.

What gets added

  • Resort fee. A nightly charge covering facilities such as pools, fitness centre, shuttles and wifi. Charged whether or not you use any of it.
  • Village or community assessment. A smaller per night charge applied in some resort villages.
  • Cleaning fee. Usually one-off rather than nightly, and larger on bigger units.
  • Taxes. Lodging taxes are a percentage, so they scale with everything above.
  • Parking. Frequently separate, and rarely free during a peak week.
  • Platform service fee. Added by the booking site, not the property. Avoided entirely by booking direct.

Why it distorts comparisons

A fee structure that is flat per night affects a cheap unit proportionally more than an expensive one. Two options that look twenty dollars apart on the nightly rate can land much closer together, or swap places entirely, once everything is included.

This is not hypothetical. It is the single most common reason people feel they overpaid for a ski week, and it is entirely avoidable with one question.

The question to ask every time

"What is the total I will pay for these exact dates, including every fee, charge and tax?" Ask it of every option before comparing any of them. A property that cannot answer quickly is telling you something useful.

Fees are not automatically bad

A resort fee that covers pools, a gym and shuttles you will actually use during a ski week is a reasonable way to price a resort. The objection is to discovering it at checkout, not to its existence.

Equally, a low rate with high fees is not automatically worse than a high rate with none. Only the total tells you which is which.

Put it in the budget

Across seven nights, a nightly fee is seven times larger than it looks on the booking page. Fold it into the lodging line of your trip budget rather than treating it as an extra.

Common questions

What is a resort fee?

A nightly charge covering shared facilities such as pools, a fitness centre, shuttles and wifi. It is charged regardless of whether you use them, and it is standard across much of the resort industry.

Do Park City rentals charge resort fees?

Many do, often alongside a village or community assessment. They are normal, but they are not always shown until checkout, so ask for the all-in total for your dates before you book.

How do I compare lodging prices fairly?

Ask every option for one number: the total for your exact dates including all fees, charges and tax. Nightly rates are not comparable because fee structures differ between properties.
