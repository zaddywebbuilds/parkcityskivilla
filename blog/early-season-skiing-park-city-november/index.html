Early Season Skiing in Park City: What November Really Gives You

Published 16 November 2026 Park City, Utah 7 min read

Nearly everybody writing about early season skiing in Park City is selling November nights, which makes the honest version hard to find. Here it is: a few runs, mostly manufactured snow, rocks near the surface, and a mountain almost entirely to yourself.

How much of the mountain is actually open in November?

There is no single percentage, and anybody quoting one is inventing it. Open terrain depends on the resort, the snowpack and the exact date, and mid-November is usually the sparsest point of the operating season.

What opens is called the opening package: a small set of groomed runs on snowmaking coverage, served by a handful of lifts. This season Park City opened on nine lifts and six named runs — two of them at Canyons Village — out of roughly 7,300 acres, more than 330 trails and over 40 lifts. That is the scale of it.

The comparison that makes it concrete is Christmas. By 25 December a typical year has Alta around 96 per cent open, Steamboat around 93 and Aspen Mountain around 80. November is nowhere near those figures — it is the difference between skiing a mountain and skiing a few runs on it. The opening day guide lists exactly which lifts and runs opened first this season.

What thin cover does to the skiing

Early-season snow sits on ground that has not been buried yet, and that changes the hazard picture rather than just the quality.

  • Rocks, stumps and roots close to the surface. A thin pack hides sharp rock and buried timber that would be metres down in February. Resorts running early-season terrain post warnings about exactly this, including unmarked obstacles and the occasional patch of open water.
  • Take skis you do not mind damaging. Core shots and destroyed edges are routine in November. This is what rock skis are for, and if you only own one good pair, that is an argument for waiting.
  • Stay on the groomed, marked runs. The edges of a trail and anything ungroomed are where the damage happens. Off-piste is not an option worth considering this early.
  • Manufactured snow skis differently. It is denser and firmer than natural snow, it holds an edge well when freshly groomed and it turns slick and fast once skied off. Expect a hard surface rather than a soft one.

None of this makes November unskiable. It makes it a different activity from the week most people are picturing when they book a ski holiday, and the packing list changes accordingly.

Where the snowpack actually stands this November

Utah started slowly. Statewide snowpack was running at roughly 27 per cent of normal across 154 measuring stations at the beginning of November. Storms moved through on 13 to 15 November, with Park City forecast to pick up four to six inches from that system, so the trend improved — from a low base.

That last row was the honest answer in mid-November: the resort was not publishing a base at all. Then the weather turned. Storms through 19 to 22 November dropped 38 inches on the resort, the cold that came with them gave snowmaking its best stretch of the autumn, and the opening was confirmed for 25 November after a five-day delay. A 27 per cent start followed by three feet in four days is a normal Utah November, and March is still where the pack reaches its deepest.

What November does better than February

Three things, and they are real.

Nobody is there. The runs that are open are empty midweek, lift queues barely exist and you can ski laps without stopping. For a confident skier who wants mileage on groomed snow, that is genuinely enjoyable.

The village is calm. Restaurants take walk-ins, the parking structure is not full and nothing needs booking weeks out. Compare that with the queueing and reservations of a February peak week.

It costs less. Lodging in late November is among the cheapest of the operating season, and that is not an accident — it is priced for what you get. If the price is the reason you are looking at November, that logic is worth following all the way through, which the guide to booking peak weeks does.

Who should ski Park City in November, and who should wait

Worth it: pass holders already in Utah or within a short drive; confident intermediates and better who are happy lapping groomers; anyone for whom the first day of the season is an event in itself; and people with rock skis and no expectations.

Wait: families whose one ski trip of the year this is; beginners who need a full ski school programme; anyone travelling a long way or flying in; groups large enough that the trip needs to work for everybody. A group of eight that travels for a weekend of four open runs will not do it twice.

The hardest case is the family with young children. November ski school runs on limited terrain, and a beginner on a crowded single learning area in hard manufactured snow has a worse first day than the same child in March.

How November compares with a week worth travelling for

Set against the two weeks we actually rent, November loses on nearly everything that matters to a travelling group. Presidents' Week in February buys the most dependable conditions of the season. Late March sits close to the snowpack peak with longer, warmer days. November buys a handful of runs and an empty car park.

That is not an argument against skiing in November. It is an argument against flying across a country to do it. Full season dates and the shape of each month are in the 2026-27 season guide.

If you are going anyway

Check the resort terrain report the morning of, not the week before. Bring old skis. Plan short days. Keep the trip cheap enough that a bad snow week does not matter, and save the proper week for February or late March.

Common questions

How much terrain is open in Park City in November?

Only the opening package: a small set of groomed runs on snowmaking coverage served by a few lifts, out of roughly 7,300 acres and more than 330 trails. There is no fixed percentage, and it depends on the snowpack and the exact date.

Is early season skiing in Park City worth it?

It is worth it for pass holders nearby, confident skiers happy to lap groomers, and anyone who treats opening weekend as an occasion. It is not worth a long journey, a family trip or a group holiday, because very little of the mountain is open.

What are the risks of skiing on thin early season cover?

Rocks, stumps, roots and unmarked obstacles sit close to the surface, and some resorts also warn about open water. Ski damage is common, which is why regulars use rock skis. Stay on groomed, marked runs and avoid trail edges and ungroomed terrain.

How deep is the snow at Park City in mid-November 2026?

Park City Mountain published no base depth through mid-November. Utah snowpack was around 27 per cent of normal at the start of the month, and on 15 November neighbouring resorts reported roughly 22 inches at Alta, 21 at Snowbird, 14 at Brighton and 8 at Solitude. Storms through 19 to 22 November then dropped 38 inches on Park City, which is what allowed the resort to open on 25 November.

Is November a good time to bring children skiing in Park City?

Generally no. Ski school runs on limited terrain in November, and a beginner learning on hard manufactured snow in a single crowded area has a worse first day than the same child in late March, when the base is deep and the mornings are warmer.
