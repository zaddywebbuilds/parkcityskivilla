Off the Mountain

Park City for Non-Skiers and Days Off the Mountain

Published 5 October 2026 Park City, Utah 5 min read

Somebody is going to stop skiing before the week is over. A tired child, a sore knee, a storm day, or somebody who never intended to ski at all. Plan for it early.

Groups that treat a rest day as a failure end up with somebody sitting in the condo feeling left out. Groups that plan for one get a better week out of the same lift tickets. With small children that day is close to inevitable, which our spring break with kids guide plans around.

On site, without organising anything

The easiest non-skiing day is the one that needs no transport and no reservation. Everything in this section is a walk from the door.

  • Heated outdoor pools and hot tubs. The most used facility on any ski trip, and the best thing for tired legs on day four. They also solve the awkward gap between the lifts closing and dinner.
  • Spa treatments. Book ahead during a holiday week, because these fill in the same way restaurants do. A massage booked on the morning you want it, in the middle of Presidents' Week, is optimistic.
  • A gym and yoga studio. Used mostly by people trying to undo what three days of skiing did to them, and by the one person in the group who cannot sit still.
  • An arcade or games room. Solves the late afternoon with teenagers better than anything else, and costs nothing to organise.

A resort with these on site means a non-skiing day needs no planning at all, which is the whole point of it being easy. If the day turns into an evening, what the village offers after the lifts close is a short walk further.

Off site, with a little planning

Park City has a proper town attached to it, which many ski resorts do not. Main Street covers an afternoon comfortably with galleries, shops and a long lunch, and it is the standard answer to a storm day. The Park City Museum sits on Main Street itself and is the one indoor stop that explains why the town looks the way it does, with mining-era exhibits that reportedly include a mine rail car and an old jail cell.

Beyond the town, the valley carries the usual mountain options and a few that exist because of the 2002 Winter Olympics. The table below is the shortlist that comes up repeatedly in local guides. Treat the detail as a starting point and confirm it with the operator, because winter operations change between seasons and several of these are weather-dependent.

The Olympic facilities, specifically

Most guides mention "the Olympic facilities" and leave it there, which is not much use when you are trying to fill a Tuesday. Utah Olympic Park is the venue, it is a short drive from Canyons Village, and it does three distinct things for a visitor who is not skiing.

There is the bobsled: a ride on the track built for the 2002 Games, run by a driver, which is the single most memorable thing a non-skier can do in Park City. There are six Nordic ski jumps, used in winter by athletes and club jumpers, so there is usually something to watch from the ground. And there is the Alf Engen Ski Museum, reported to be free to enter, which is the low-commitment option on a cold day.

The park also runs tubing on long sliding lanes. One detail worth knowing for a large party: groups of twenty or more are reported to qualify for discounted activity passes, which matters if you are travelling as the sort of multi-family group that fills more than one villa. Confirm the current season's activities, hours and prices with the park directly before you build a day around them.

For somebody who is not skiing at all

If one member of the group never intended to ski, the arithmetic changes. They do not need a lift ticket, which is a substantial saving across a week, and they do need somewhere pleasant to spend the day.

That makes the quality of the accommodation matter more than it does for the skiers, who are out from nine until four. A unit with a proper living room, a kitchen and a view is doing real work in that situation, and a hotel room with two beds and a window is not. It is the clearest case for a villa over a room, and it is why the layout of the unit is worth looking at properly before you book.

The practical version: the non-skier gets the keys, the car if there is one, and the job of being somewhere warm at four o'clock when everybody else comes in cold and hungry.

What to book ahead and what to leave loose

Anything with a vehicle, a guide or a timed slot needs booking in a peak week. That means bobsled, tubing, sleigh rides, guided snowshoe tours and spa treatments. Anything that is just a building you walk into, including the museum and Main Street itself, can wait until the morning you want it.

Book the weather-dependent things with a cancellation policy you have actually read. A sleigh ride in a storm is cancelled by the operator, not by you, and the terms decide whether that costs you anything.

Plan it on day one

Decide in advance which day is the non-skiing day, and book anything that needs booking for it. Left to chance it lands on the worst weather day of the week, when everybody else has had the same idea and every indoor option in town is full.

Common questions

What is there to do in Park City if you do not ski?

Spa, heated pools, a gym, and the town itself. Main Street has restaurants, galleries, shops and the Park City Museum, and the valley offers tubing, guided snowshoeing, sleigh rides and Utah Olympic Park, where you can ride the bobsled track built for the 2002 Games.

Should I plan a rest day on a ski week?

Yes. Most groups need one by day four or five, and planning it means it lands on a day you chose rather than the worst weather day of the week, when every indoor option in town is busy.

Do non-skiers need a lift ticket?

No, which is a substantial saving across a week. It does make the quality of the accommodation matter more, since they will spend far more of the day in it than the skiers will.

What can you do at Utah Olympic Park in winter?

Ride the bobsled track built for the 2002 Games, watch training on the six Nordic ski jumps, go tubing on the long sliding lanes, and visit the Alf Engen Ski Museum. Confirm the current season's activities and hours with the park before planning a day around them.

Which non-skiing activities need booking in advance?

Anything with a guide, a vehicle or a timed slot: bobsled, tubing, sleigh rides, guided snowshoe tours and spa treatments. Main Street and the museum do not need booking, though a dinner table for a group in a holiday week does.
