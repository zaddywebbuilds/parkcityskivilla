Packing for a Week in Park City

Published 7 October 2026 Park City, Utah 6 min read

Most ski packing lists are too long. The short version is that a few items decide whether the week goes well, and laundry in the accommodation changes everything else.

A seven night ski trip for a family generates a lot of wet clothing. How you handle that determines how much you need to bring.

Laundry changes the list

If the unit has a washer and dryer, pack for about three days and wash twice. That is the difference between one bag per person and two, and it matters when you are also carrying ski gear.

It also solves the daily problem, which is wet gloves and damp base layers. Drying those properly overnight is the thing that makes day four comfortable.

The items that actually matter

  1. Gloves, and a spare pair each. The single highest-value item on the list, particularly for children. Cold hands end a ski day faster than anything else and wet gloves do not dry by morning without help.
  2. Proper base layers. Merino or synthetic. Cotton holds moisture and makes you cold.
  3. Goggles, with a spare lens or a low-light lens. Flat light and falling snow are normal in February, and sunglasses do not work in either.
  4. Sunscreen and lip balm with sun protection. At altitude on snow the sun is much stronger than the temperature suggests. Day one sunburn is a Park City classic.
  5. Thick socks, one pair per day, and no more layers than that. Two pairs of socks in a ski boot makes feet colder, not warmer.
  6. A warm hat and a neck gaiter. Small, light, and the difference on a cold morning.

What to rent rather than carry

Skis, boards and helmets are almost always better rented than flown. Airline ski bag fees, the risk of damage in transit and the inconvenience of hauling them through an airport rarely beat renting at the resort.

Boots are the exception. If you ski more than about a week a year, owning boots that fit is the best equipment decision available. More in the rentals guide.

Off the mountain

Less than people pack. Warm layers, proper boots with grip for icy pavements, swimwear for the hot tub, and one outfit for the evening you go into town. Park City is not a dressy place.

The three easy mistakes

Packing one pair of gloves. Forgetting sunscreen because it is February. And packing seven days of clothing into a unit that has a washing machine.

If you are flying with children

Put one complete set of ski clothing for each child in a carry-on bag. Delayed luggage on a seven night trip is survivable. Delayed luggage with no gloves and a lesson booked for nine o'clock the next morning is not.

Common questions

What should I pack for a ski week in Park City?

Gloves with a spare pair each, proper base layers, goggles with a low-light lens, sunscreen and lip balm, thick socks, and a warm hat. If the unit has laundry, pack about three days of clothing rather than seven.

Should I bring my own skis to Park City?

Usually not. Airline ski bag fees and transit damage rarely beat renting at the resort. Boots are the exception if you ski regularly, because fit matters and rental boots cause most bad days.

Do I need special clothing for February in Park City?

Warm layers and genuinely waterproof gloves. February mornings are cold, and sun protection matters more than the temperature suggests because of the altitude and the reflection off snow.
