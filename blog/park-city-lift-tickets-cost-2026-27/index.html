What a Week of Park City Lift Access Costs in 2026-27

Published 23 November 2026 Park City, Utah 7 min read

Most guides to Park City lift tickets for 2026-27 were priced in September and have not been touched since. One of the better-ranking ones still says an Epic Pass pays for itself in about four peak days, on figures where its own arithmetic gives three. Here are the numbers as they stand on 23 November, and the deadline that matters more.

The deadline that beats every price

Before any arithmetic: 6 December 2026 is the last day to buy an Epic Pass for this season. Vail Resorts said so in its own announcement on 16 November. After that date the pass products come off sale entirely and do not come back, whatever the snow does in January.

That is why every comparison below has a clock on it. If you are weighing a pass against tickets for a February or March trip, you are not choosing at leisure — you are choosing within about two weeks of reading this.

What a single day at Park City actually costs

Here is the thing no competitor guide admits: Park City Mountain does not publish a single-day price. Tickets are sold dynamically, by date, so the number you see depends on which day you pick and how far ahead you buy. There is no window rate pinned to a page for the season.

What exists instead is reporting on specific dates. Local coverage in September put a peak-day adult window ticket around $369, and figures published in August gave roughly $325 online for a peak date, up to about $364 at the window. A check of the resort calendar in mid-November found peak dates near $325 online and opening weekend far cheaper, around $202 ahead or $224 on the day.

So the honest answer is a range, not a price: roughly $200 on a quiet early-season day and roughly $325 to $369 on a peak February or Christmas day, with the cheaper number almost always going to whoever booked first. The break-even, done properly

This is where the stale guides fall over. One well-ranked Park City lift ticket guide says an Epic Pass pays for itself in “about four” peak days, using a pass price of $1,145 against a $369 ticket. That division gives 3.1, and the price it used is last season’s. Both halves are wrong, which is reason enough to do it yourself.

Three to four peak days, then, depending on the product and the age of the skier. That is a lower bar than most people assume, and it is why the new age discount matters: a 28-year-old can cross the line a day earlier than their parents.

The right product for a single week

Now the part the pass-versus-ticket framing hides. If you are flying in for one week and will not return this season, the product to look at is not the full Epic Pass. It is the Epic Day Pass, one to seven days at $50 to $106 each.

Seven days at the top of that range is $742. The full Epic Pass is $1,245. Unless you are returning for a second trip, or skiing another Epic resort this winter, the day pass wins comfortably — and the guides that compare only passes against window tickets never put that comparison in front of you. The cheaper per-day rates are the versions without holiday access, so check the dates your week falls on.

The blackout dates to check before anything else

The Epic Local Pass is the obvious saving at $286 less than the full pass, and for most of the winter it behaves identically at Park City. On four occasions it does not. Epic’s own restricted dates page lists these peak restricted dates for 2026-27, on which the Epic Local Pass cannot be used at Park City:

  • 27 and 28 November 2026 — the Friday and Saturday of the resort’s own opening weekend
  • 26 to 31 December 2026 — the Christmas week
  • 16 January 2027 — the Martin Luther King weekend Saturday
  • 13 and 14 February 2027 — the first two days of Presidents’ Week

That first entry deserves a moment. The resort opened on 25 November, so Epic Local holders are locked out on the Friday and Saturday of opening weekend itself. The last entry catches families: it is the opening weekend of Presidents’ Week at Canyons Village. The full Epic Pass has no blackout dates. Some pass guides say Local holders can buy discounted tickets on restricted dates, but Epic’s own page does not, so confirm rather than count on it. None of it applies at Deer Valley, which sits on Ikon rather than Epic and does not open until 5 December.

Common questions

When do Epic Pass sales close for the 2026-27 season?

After 6 December 2026. Vail Resorts announced the date on 16 November, and the pass products come off sale for the whole season rather than returning at a higher price. Lift tickets remain available after that, bought by the day at dynamic prices.

How much does an Epic Pass cost for 2026-27?

Vail Resorts published $1,245 for an adult Epic Pass and $959 for an adult Epic Local Pass, with a new 20 per cent discount for ages 13 to 30 bringing those to $995 and $759. The Epic Day Pass runs from $50 to $106 a day for one to seven days. Confirm at epicpass.com, because the ladder moved during November.

How many days do you need to ski for an Epic Pass to pay for itself?

Three to four peak days at this season's prices. An adult Epic Pass at $1,245 breaks even at 3.4 days against a $369 peak window ticket, so four days. An adult Epic Local at $959 breaks even at 2.6 days, so three. Guides quoting about four days for every product are using last season's pass price.

What is a single-day lift ticket at Park City Mountain?

There is no fixed price. Park City Mountain sells tickets dynamically by date, so the figure depends on the day and how far ahead you buy. Reported figures for this season range from roughly $200 on a quiet early-season day to about $325 booked ahead or $369 at the window on a peak day.

Which dates can an Epic Local Pass not be used at Park City?

Epic lists four peak restricted periods for 2026-27: 27 and 28 November 2026, 26 to 31 December 2026, 16 January 2027, and 13 and 14 February 2027. The full Epic Pass has no blackout dates and is unrestricted on all of them.
