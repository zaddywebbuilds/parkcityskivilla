The Red Pine Gondola: The Lift Your Canyons Village Week Runs On

Published 26 September 2026 Park City, Utah 6 min read

If you stay in Canyons Village, one lift shapes your mornings. Knowing what it connects to, and when it backs up, is most of what you need to ski the week well.

On this page
  1. What it does
  2. The other lifts in the village
  3. When it queues
  4. Getting back at the end of the day

Canyons Village is not laid out around a street. It is laid out around the bottom of the Red Pine Gondola, and almost everything in the village is described by its distance from that point, including the lodging.

What it does

The Red Pine Gondola carries you out of the village and up onto the mountain, where the rest of the lift network opens up. For most people staying in the village it is the first lift of the day, every day.

The base ticket office sits at the bottom of it, which matters on the first morning. Collecting passes is a walk rather than a drive, and you can do it while somebody else is still getting boots on.

The other lifts in the village

Two more are worth knowing by name.

  • The Orange Bubble Express. A heated chairlift with a clear cover that pulls down over you. It was the first of its kind in the country and it is the lift everyone remembers from the trip, particularly on a cold morning.
  • The Sunrise Gondola. Also in the village, and a useful alternative when the Red Pine queue is doing its nine o'clock thing.

Having more than one way up out of the same village is a real advantage during a peak week. When one base lift backs up, the other usually has not yet.

When it queues

The pattern is predictable and easy to exploit. The village lifts are quiet when they open, build steadily from about half past nine, and peak somewhere between ten and eleven as everyone who drove in finishes parking.

The people staying in the village have a structural advantage here, and most of them waste it. Being on the first gondola of the day costs you nothing if you are already a three minute walk away, and it buys an hour of near-empty runs.

The one habit worth building

Ski the first hour hard, take a long lunch while the mountain is at its worst, then go back out. During a holiday week this is the difference between a good day and a day spent in lift queues.

Getting back at the end of the day

Returning to Canyons Village on snow is straightforward from most of the mountain, and signage is good. Give yourself more time than you think on the last run of the day, because the routes back to the village are the ones everybody else is also taking at four o'clock.

Common questions

What is the main lift out of Canyons Village?

The Red Pine Gondola. The village is built around its base, and the Canyons base ticket office sits at the bottom of it.

What is the Orange Bubble Express?

A heated chairlift in Canyons Village with a clear cover that pulls down over the seat. It was the first heated and covered chairlift in the United States.

What time should I be at the lift during a busy week?

At opening if you can manage it. The village lifts are quiet for the first hour and busiest between ten and eleven once the day visitors have parked.
