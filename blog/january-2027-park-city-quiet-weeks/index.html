Park City in January 2027: Which Weeks Are Quiet and Which Are Not

Published 25 November 2026 Park City, Utah 8 min read

January in Park City has always divided neatly between the quiet weeks after New Year’s and the louder ones closer to February. In 2027 that division is sharper than usual. Sundance Film Festival has left for Boulder, taking the crowd that filled the third week of January for four decades. What stays are the Snow League halfpipe competition on 21–23 January and the Deer Valley World Cup on 28–30. Here is what each week looks like on the ground.

How January 2027 divides week by week

The easiest way to see January 2027 is to lay it out as a table. The holiday spike that ends December carries briefly into the first few days of January; from there, the first three weeks are some of the quietest and most affordable skiing on the Park City calendar. Two back-to-back events in the final ten days change that picture — not to Presidents’ Week intensity, but enough to notice on lift lines and in restaurant wait times.

Early January: quietest skiing of the season

The first working week of January is as uncrowded as Park City gets. New Year’s visitors have cleared out, schools are back in session, and no events fill the calendar until Martin Luther King weekend. Historically this period sees the lowest lift-line wait times of the season, the most available restaurant walk-ins, and the softest hotel rates of any ski week.

Hotel bookings for early January 2027 were below the prior year as of a KPCW report from October 2026, which the station attributed partly to the Sundance departure. That is unusual context: in prior years, Sundance’s third-week presence created overflow into the surrounding weeks. With the festival in Boulder, there is no such downstream effect in 2027. Early January is simply quiet.

The skiing itself is typically solid. The base from November and December is built, and January in Utah is usually a good month for new snow. The full 2026–27 season guide tracks conditions as the winter develops. Early January is not the season’s best-conditions week — that is February and March — but terrain coverage is generally full and the snowpack is building toward its peak.

Mid-January: still quiet, with one blackout day

The second full week of January follows the same pattern as the first, with one exception: 16 January 2027 (Martin Luther King Jr. Day) is a blackout day on the Epic Local Pass. If you hold an Epic Local Pass and plan to ski that date, you will need a single-day lift ticket. The rest of the week is unrestricted. Epic Pass holders have no January blackouts at Park City at all.

The MLK weekend itself (15–17 January) draws a modest domestic crowd, mostly from Utah and the Intermountain West. It is not a major destination weekend on the scale of Presidents’ Week or New Year’s, but Saturday and Sunday are noticeably busier than the surrounding midweeks. If your January trip includes that weekend, build in earlier morning starts on the 16th and 17th. The Monday blackout on Epic Local means those lift lines will be shorter than you might expect, since a share of skiers with passes choose to ski the unrestricted days on either side instead.

One thing that will be absent in mid-January: the Sundance Film Festival activity that used to run from roughly the 15th to the 25th. No celebrities on Main Street, no industry crowd, no inflated taxi wait times. The January 2027 events guide covers what is happening in its place, including the Park City Jazz Foundation concert series and the Utah Symphony performances at Kimball Arts Center.

The Snow League: 21–23 January 2027

The Snow League is a halfpipe and slopestyle series run by World Surf League that draws the same pool of professional athletes as the X Games. Its Park City stop in January 2027 is confirmed for 21–23 January. The competition takes place at the town base area of Park City Mountain Resort, specifically in Scott’s Bowl near the Eagle and Three Kings lifts. This is a critical logistical note for anyone staying at Canyons Village: the halfpipe is on the Park City Mountain side of the resort, not at Canyons Village.

To reach the event from Canyons Village, take the Quicksilver gondola across to the Park City Mountain base. The ride takes around fifteen minutes and runs continuously during resort hours. Free transit between Canyons Village and the Town Lift area runs throughout the day during ski season; check the Park City Transit schedule for specifics. Driving is not recommended on competition days, when the town base parking lots fill early. The Snow League lodging guide includes notes on where to stay for easiest access to the event venue.

The event is free to watch from the viewing area at the base. Athletes compete on the same halfpipe that hosted the 2019 FIS Freestyle World Championships. The format runs heats on the 21st and 22nd, with finals on the 23rd. Expect the base area to be significantly more crowded than a normal January weekend; the lift lines on the Town and Three Kings side will be longer than usual, so plan ski days around the event rather than fighting the crowds. Skiing on the Canyons Village side is largely unaffected.

Deer Valley World Cup: 28–30 January 2027

One week after the Snow League ends, the Deer Valley World Cup brings FIS alpine racing to Park City. The venue is Deer Valley Resort’s Stein Eriksen slope, which has hosted FIS events for years. Racing takes place over three days: 28, 29 and 30 January 2027, with women’s slalom and giant slalom as the featured events.

Daytime racing is free to spectators. Access to the race venue during competition hours requires no ticket. Evening access after 5 pm does require a free digital ticket reserved ahead through Deer Valley’s event page; walk-in capacity on the evening of the finals is limited, and in prior years the free tickets were claimed within a day or two of going live. Reserve early. For more detail on watching and getting to the race, the Deer Valley World Cup 2027 guide covers the schedule, spectator access, and parking.

The World Cup affects Park City Mountain more lightly than the Snow League does. Deer Valley and Park City Mountain share a transit corridor, and parking in the Deer Valley area fills on race days, but the main Park City Mountain terrain is not directly impacted by the event crowd. If your trip overlaps the World Cup weekend, skiing at Park City Mountain on the race days is a reasonable way to avoid the Deer Valley surge while still being in town for the event atmosphere in the evenings.

What January means for Presidents’ Week planning

The quiet January described above is relevant to a February booking in one specific way: it makes an early President’s Week look better by comparison. Skiers who travel in early January often note that the terrain is fully open and the lines are short, but that mid-February at full base and peak snowpack is worth planning ahead for — and that the January experience builds the case for returning.

Presidents’ Week 2027 runs 13–20 February. It is the busiest week of the Park City ski season and historically books months ahead for the most desirable properties. By the time January 2027’s quiet weeks arrive, the remaining available lodging for February will be thin. How far ahead to book Presidents’ Week 2027 gives the full booking timeline. The broader peak-week guide shows how Presidents’ Week compares to Christmas and spring break on the booking curve.

The villa at Canyons Village is available for Presidents’ Week 2027 (13–20 February) and spring break (21–28 March). It is not available in January. If the quiet January weeks are what you are looking for, the guide to what is happening in Park City in January 2027 gives the full events picture without the lodging angle.

Common questions

Is early January 2027 quiet in Park City?

Yes. The first two weeks of January are typically the quietest skiing of the Park City season. New Year's visitors leave by January 3 or 4, and no major events fill the calendar until the Snow League on January 21-23. Hotel bookings for early January 2027 were below the prior year as of KPCW's October 2026 report, confirming the pattern. For skiers who can travel mid-week in January, it is as uncrowded as the season gets.

Where does the Snow League halfpipe take place in Park City?

The Snow League halfpipe competition is at the town base area, in Scott's Bowl near the Eagle and Three Kings lifts at Park City Mountain Resort. It is not at Canyons Village. Groups staying at Canyons Village should cross via the Quicksilver gondola to the Park City Mountain side, or use the free transit shuttle rather than driving.

Is the Deer Valley World Cup free to watch?

Daytime racing at Deer Valley World Cup (January 28-30, 2027) is free to watch from the race venue. Evening entry after 5 pm requires a free digital ticket reserved ahead through Deer Valley's event page; walk-in capacity is limited. Arrive early for the best standing positions on the course.

Does January 2027 have any Epic Local Pass blackout days?

Only one day in January 2027 falls under an Epic Local Pass blackout: January 16, 2027 (Martin Luther King Day). The rest of the month is unrestricted on the Epic Local Pass. Epic Pass holders have no January blackouts at Park City Mountain. If you plan to ski January 16 on an Epic Local Pass, you will need a single-day lift ticket for that date.

Is Sundance Film Festival still in Park City in January 2027?

No. Sundance Film Festival moved to Boulder, Colorado starting in 2027, ending a 40-plus year run in Park City. January 2027 is the first January in four decades without Sundance in town. This removes a significant crowd and lodging pressure that previously hit the third week of January; Park City's January 2027 is quieter than any recent January on that dimension.
