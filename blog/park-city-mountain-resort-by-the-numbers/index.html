Park City Mountain Resort by the Numbers, and What They Mean on Snow

Published 26 September 2026 Park City, Utah 6 min read

Park City is the largest ski resort in the country, which sounds like marketing until you try to cross it in a day. The numbers matter because they change how you plan.

On this page
  1. What the size actually changes
  2. Two bases, one mountain
  3. Terrain, honestly
  4. Altitude

The headline figures are roughly 7,300 acres of skiable terrain, more than 330 trails and 41 lifts. It hosted events at the 2002 Winter Olympics. It is, by skiable acreage, the biggest resort in the United States.

What the size actually changes

Three practical things follow from the scale, and none of them are obvious from a trail map.

  • You cannot ski it all in a week, so stop trying. Groups that attempt a grand tour every day spend most of the week on traverses and cat tracks getting between areas. Pick a sector a day.
  • Getting back matters. From the far side of the mountain, returning to your base takes real time. Know your last-lift deadline, and treat it seriously with children in the group.
  • Crowds spread out. Even during a holiday week, the lift queues concentrate at the two bases and the main arteries. Twenty minutes from a base, the mountain feels half empty.

Two bases, one mountain

The resort reached its current size by joining two previously separate mountains, which is why it has two base areas about ten minutes apart by road. One ticket covers both, and you can ride between them on snow.

For a visitor the useful consequence is that you can stay at the quieter base and still ski the whole thing, which is the argument for Canyons Village in one sentence.

Terrain, honestly

Park City is strongest for beginners, intermediates and families. The groomed intermediate terrain is extensive and genuinely good, and the learning areas are well set up.

Strong skiers sometimes find it less steep than its size implies. There is challenging terrain, particularly in the bowls and the trees, but nobody books Park City for the steeps alone. If that is what you are after, plan a day at another Utah resort as part of the trip.

Altitude

The base sits around 6,800 to 7,000 feet, and the lifts take you well above 9,000. Most visitors arrive from close to sea level, and the first day tends to feel harder than it should. Planning for that is more useful than being surprised by it.

Common questions

How big is Park City Mountain Resort?

Roughly 7,300 acres of skiable terrain with more than 330 trails and 41 lifts, which makes it the largest ski resort in the United States by skiable acreage.

Can you ski the whole resort in a day?

Not meaningfully. Crossing the mountain takes a large part of a day and leaves little time to actually ski. Most people get more out of it by picking one sector each day.

Is Park City good for advanced skiers?

It has challenging terrain in the bowls and the trees, but its strength is intermediate and family skiing. Advanced skiers often add a day at another Utah resort during the same trip.
