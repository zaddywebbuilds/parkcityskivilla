What a Park City Ski Week Actually Costs for a Family of Eight

Published 2 October 2026 Park City, Utah 7 min read

Most ski trip budgets fail in the same two places. Here is the full set of lines for a seven night Park City week, including the ones that do not appear until checkout.

On this page
  1. The lines, largest first
  2. The two lines people leave out
  3. Where the savings actually are
  4. What not to economise on

This is a structure rather than a quote. Prices move every season and vary enormously by week, so the useful thing is knowing which lines exist and roughly how big each one is relative to the others.

The two lines people leave out

Food for eight, for seven days

Three meals a day for eight people across a week is somewhere between a significant expense and the largest single line after lodging, depending entirely on whether you have a kitchen.

Breakfast is the clearest example. Eight breakfasts out, seven days running, is a number that would change which lodging you booked if you had put it in the comparison. The kitchen guide works through this.

Fees that appear at checkout

Nightly resort fees, village assessments, cleaning charges and tax are all normal and all easy to miss while comparing nightly rates. Across seven nights they are not a rounding error. See the guide to resort fees.

The only comparison that works

For every option you are considering, get one number: the all-in total for your exact dates with every fee and tax included. Then add a realistic food budget for your group under that option. Compare those two figures and nothing else.

Where the savings actually are

  1. Book the lodging early. For a peak week this is the largest single saving available, and it is only available early.
  2. Buy lift access in advance. Multiply the per-day difference by eight people and seven days before deciding it is not worth the effort.
  3. Get a kitchen and use it. Breakfast and a few dinners in is the difference between budgets, not a small economy.
  4. Divide the transfer. A private van looks expensive until you divide it by eight, at which point it often beats the shared shuttle.
  5. Book direct where you can. Platform service fees on a peak-week booking are substantial. See booking direct.

What not to economise on

Proximity to the lift, with children in the group, and decent gloves. Both are small relative to the trip and both determine whether people are still enjoying it on day five.

Common questions

What are the biggest costs of a Park City ski trip?

Lodging first, lift access second, then food. Equipment rental, transfers, resort fees, lessons and parking make up the rest. Food is the most controllable of the large lines, because a kitchen changes it completely.

What costs do people forget to budget for?

Feeding the group for a week, and the fees that appear at checkout such as nightly resort fees, village assessments, cleaning and tax. Both are substantial across seven nights for eight people.

How can I reduce the cost of a peak-week ski trip?

Book lodging early, buy lift access in advance, use a kitchen rather than eating every meal out, divide a private transfer across the group, and book direct to avoid platform service fees.
