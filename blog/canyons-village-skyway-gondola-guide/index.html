The Canyons Village Skyway Gondola: What Replaced the Cabriolet, and What It Changes

Published 28 October 2026 Park City, Utah 7 min read

A lot of the Canyons Village guides still online describe a lift that no longer exists. The Canyons Village Skyway gondola replaced the Cabriolet for the 2026-27 season, and the difference is bigger than a new coat of paint.

If you are reading other guides to Canyons Village, check their dates first. Several pages still ranking for this village describe the Cabriolet as a current lift, and one widely linked guide is around five years old. The Canyons Village Skyway gondola is the biggest change to this base in a long time, and worth understanding before you book a week here.

What the Skyway replaced

The Cabriolet was unusual, and most people who rode it remember it. It was an open car with no seats. You stood up, held a rail, and rode uphill in whatever weather was happening. In October it was charming. In a February wind at seven in the morning, with children and gear, it was a test of character. It ran for close to three decades and made its final trip in March 2026.

The Skyway does the same job with the opposite philosophy. It is a ten-person enclosed cabin with floor-to-ceiling glass, so you are sitting down and out of the weather for the whole ride. Like the Cabriolet, it is free to ride. You do not need a lift ticket or a pass to get on, because it is not a ski lift. It is village transport.

Two other details are worth having. The line runs roughly 2,700 feet and climbs a little under 200 feet, connecting the new Canyons Village parking structure near the Frostwood Drive roundabout up to the village base. And unlike the Cabriolet, which ran bottom to top with nothing in between, the Skyway includes a mid-station, which the resort said was intended to improve access for homeowners and lodging properties along the line.

Which lift does what

This is where the stale guides do real damage, because they leave people expecting the wrong thing. Three different lifts get mentioned in the same breath and they are not interchangeable.

Read the bottom row twice. The Skyway is an excellent piece of kit, and it is not the lift that puts you on snow. That is still the Red Pine Gondola, or the Orange Bubble Express beside it. We set the whole base out, lift by lift, in the Canyons Village lift map.

Does the Skyway take you skiing?

No. The Skyway runs below the skiing, between the car parks and the village plaza. You ride it up, you step off into the village, and then you walk to a ski lift like everybody else standing there.

Which means the honest answer to how much the Skyway matters to your trip depends entirely on where you sleep. If you drive in for the day, it is now the best part of your morning rather than the worst. If you are staying in the village already, you may ride it twice in a week and otherwise forget it exists, because you are starting your day above it.

The same distinction trips people up when they read a listing advertising ski access. Our note on what ski-in ski-out actually means in Park City is worth reading alongside this, because both questions reduce to one: how many minutes, in boots, from your door to a lift that goes up?

What changes on a cold February morning

Here is the practical difference, and it is not trivial.

On the Cabriolet, the ride itself was part of the cold. You arrived at the village already chilled, which meant the first ten minutes of the day went on recovering rather than skiing. With a group it compounded: somebody always needed to warm up before they would get on a lift, and that somebody was usually a child.

The Skyway removes that entirely. You get in, you sit down, the glass keeps the wind off, and you arrive in the same condition you left the car. For a family travelling with young children during a peak holiday week, that is one fewer thing that can derail the morning.

The thing nobody mentions

Free to ride does not mean free to park. Park City Mountain ended free parking in the Canyons Village garage and now charges a daily rate. If your plan was to drive over each morning and ride up, price that in. Check the resort's current parking page, because the rules and the hour-based pricing change between seasons. Park City Mountain has the 2026-27 winter running from 20 November to 19 April.

If you are weighing whether to have a car here at all, we went through that in detail in do you need a car in Park City. The short version is that the answer has shifted further towards no now that parking costs money, and further towards no again if you are staying inside the village.

Where it loads, and which lodging sits closest

The Skyway loads from the new Canyons Village parking structure off SR 224 near the Frostwood Drive roundabout, and unloads at the village base beside the plaza. The mid-station sits partway up the line, serving lodging along that stretch.

The lodging that benefits most from the Skyway is, counter-intuitively, the lodging that benefits least from being in Canyons Village. If you are far enough out that you were driving and parking anyway, the Skyway is a genuine upgrade to your day. If you are already inside the village, you were never riding it to go skiing in the first place, and the thing that matters to you is your walking distance to the Red Pine Gondola instead.

Our own villa falls in the second group. From it, the Skyway is something you look at rather than something you queue for. Common questions

Is the Canyons Village Skyway free to ride?

Yes. The Skyway is free to ride, as the Cabriolet it replaced was. You do not need a lift ticket or a pass to get on it, because it is village transport rather than a ski lift.

Did the Skyway replace the Cabriolet?

Yes. The Cabriolet was an open standing car with no seats that carried people from the car parks up to the Canyons Village base for roughly three decades. It made its final run in March 2026 and the Skyway, a ten-person enclosed gondola, took over the same job.

Can you ski down to the Skyway?

No, and this is the most common misunderstanding about it. The Skyway runs between the car parks and the village, below the skiing. To get onto the mountain you take the Red Pine Gondola or the Orange Bubble Express from the village itself.

Do I need the Skyway if I am staying in Canyons Village?

Mostly no. If your lodging is already in the village you walk straight to the Red Pine Gondola and never touch the Skyway on a normal ski morning. It matters most to day visitors who park, and to guests arriving with luggage.

Does the Skyway have a mid-station?

Yes. The resort said when it announced the project that the gondola would include a mid-station to improve access for homeowners and lodging properties along the line. That is the main functional difference from the old Cabriolet, which ran bottom to top only.

Is parking still free at Canyons Village?

No. Park City Mountain ended free parking in the Canyons Village garage and set a daily rate for the 2026-27 season. Check the resort's current parking page before you drive over, because rates and the hour-based rules change.
